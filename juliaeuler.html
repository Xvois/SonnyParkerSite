JuliaEuler

JuliaEuler is an incompressible fluid dynamics solver implemented in Julia with Chorin's projection method, designed for educational purposes and experimentation with numerical methods. It was developed as an exercise in modelling physical systems in Julia and exploring numerical techniques for solving partial differential equations in fluid dynamics. The project serves as a foundation for further exploration of more complex fluid dynamics simulations and numerical methods in computational physics.

Chorin's projection method is an iterative algorithm used to solve the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. The basis of the alogirthm is Helmholtz-Hodge decomposition, which allows the velocity field to be decomposed into a divergence-free component and a gradient of a scalar potential. where $\mathbf{u}$ is the velocity field, $\mathbf{u}^*$ is an intermediate velocity field that does not satisfy the incompressibility condition, and $\phi$ is a scalar potential. The algorithm proceeds in three main steps:

  1. Compute the intermediate velocity field $\mathbf{u}^*$ by solving the momentum equations without the pressure term.
  2. Solve the Poisson equation for the scalar potential $\phi$ to enforce incompressibility: $$\nabla^2 \phi = \frac{1}{\Delta t} \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}^*$$
  3. Update the velocity field using the computed scalar potential: $$\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{u}^* - \nabla \phi$$

Julia provides simple annotations for optimisations and parallelism, which makes prototyping and testing numerical methods very easy, and the performance is often comparable to more traditional languages like C++ or Fortran without needing to write any low-level code.
